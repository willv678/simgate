SimGate study · intersection_ego_speed

At intersections with a recorded crossing car, which combinations of the ego's speed at hand-off and the crossing car's arrival time make VaVAM with the linear MPC collide or leave the road? The goal is the 5 most challenging cases, each on a different scene, confirmed by repeats.

49 simulated drives on 6 scene(s) rebuilt from real recordings, varying ego speed, actor time shift.

What the words on this page mean
  • Run: one simulated drive of a scene by the driving policy under test.
  • Scene: a few seconds of a real recorded drive, rebuilt so the policy can drive it again; named by the first characters of its id.
  • Test car (the "ego"): the car the policy drives. The other road users replay what they did in the recording.
  • Recorded drive: the path the human driver took in the recording.
  • Kept: the run passed every automatic check that the simulator ran it as asked. Only kept runs count as evidence.
  • Failed: the test car hit something with its front or side, or left the road.
  • Setting: one scene at one knob value, numbered S1, S2, … so the findings can cite it.
  • 90% range: the failure rates that fit what was seen, at 90% confidence. Few runs give a wide range.

1. The question

At intersections with a recorded crossing car, which combinations of the ego's speed at hand-off and the crossing car's arrival time make VaVAM with the linear MPC collide or leave the road? The goal is the 5 most challenging cases, each on a different scene, confirmed by repeats.

What was varied: ego speed: speed of the test car when the driving policy takes over, relative to its recording at that place; actor time shift: seconds by which the retimed road user moves later than recorded (negative: earlier).

The researcher's brief, as written

# Intersection: ego speed against the crossing car's timing ## Question In scenes where the ego crosses or turns at an intersection with crossing traffic, which combinations of the ego's speed when the driving policy takes over and the crossing car's arrival time make the policy collide or leave the road? Find the 5 most challenging cases, each on a different scene, confirmed by repeats. ## Scope Scenes tagged intersection with a recorded crossing vehicle. Vary the ego's speed at hand-off (0.6x to 1.4x its recorded speed) and retime the crossing car (class automobile): arrive up to 2 s earlier or later, at its recorded speed. Replay the other traffic as recorded, as in Euro NCAP crossing tests, where the test vehicle's speed and the target's timing are the parameters. No delay or perception error. ## Budget About 50 runs. ## What counts A front or side collision, or leaving the road. Near misses rank next.

2. The answer

The goal was not met: only 2 of the 5 cases were confirmed, on two scenes. In both, VaVAM with the linear MPC failed every run at 1.2x hand-off speed (S1 on 02eadd92 and S3 on 054b5901). The failure ranges sit above 0.5 but are wide, because each rests on only a few runs. Neither case is really about the crossing car's timing. On 054b5901 the ego ignored a RIGHT command and drove onto the median with no other car involved, whether the crossing car was shifted or not. On 02eadd92 the ego drifted left into the oncoming bus at the recorded timing; no other timing was tried there. All of these failures are flagged as possible simulator artifacts because the ego ended more than 3.5 m off the recorded path. Triage found that the policy steered off course first and the camera view was clean before each failure, so they look like the policy's errors, not rendering. Scene 13a9767e came close to the crossing car many times across timings and speeds but never failed, and the other three scenes showed nothing.

Goal, checked by code (not by the model): 2 of 5 challenging settings confirmed, after 7 rounds.

Still open

  • Whether timing matters on 02eadd92: only recorded timing was run. Runs at 1.2x with the bus shifted by about ±1 s, plus runs at 1.0x and 0.8x, would show whether the collision depends on the bus's timing or only on the left drift.
  • The speed threshold on 054b5901: repeats at 1.0x (S2 has one run) and runs at 1.1x and 1.4x would show where the departure onto the median begins.
  • Three more scenes are needed for the goal. 13a9767e is the best candidate. Repeats of its closest near misses, at recorded speed with the crossing car 1 to 2 s early, plus untried slow-speed settings such as 0.6x, could show whether any combination tips it into a collision.
  • The plan lists all six scenes as going straight, yet the triage of both failing scenes shows a RIGHT command and a recorded path curving right. The turn tags should be checked: if these scenes are right turns, the failures concern turning, not crossing traffic.
  • None of the failures was caused by the crossing car's timing. Turning across traffic, the four scenes left out, and planner delay or perception error were not covered.

7. Where this came from

The plan was written by claude-opus-5-5 from the brief and checked by code against the knob catalog and the run budget. The runs were chosen in 7 rounds by llm (claude-opus-5-5); code rejected any choice outside the plan before it ran. Failures were explained by claude-opus-5-5. The answer and findings were written by claude-opus-5-5.

Runs: 49 in the study, 49 kept, 0 not kept; 8 of the kept runs failed.

How the numbers were computed: a run counts only if it passed every automatic check of the simulator (it is kept). A run failed if the test car hit something with its front or side, or left the road. For each setting, code counts the kept runs that failed and gives the 90% Wilson range of the failure rate. A failure is flagged as possibly the simulator's when the test car was more than 3.5 m (about a lane) from the recorded drive, or a camera frame was black, because there the rebuilt scene is drawn less reliably. The model wrote the words; every count and range on this page is computed from the run records, not written by the model.

Why the plan looks the way it does

Ten scenes are tagged intersection and have a recorded crossing vehicle. Four of them already failed at the default speed and timing: 048b974e, 05a1d902, 0e02fb8c and 0f1f60d5. Three of those four failures had a closest approach above 1 m, so they were probably road departures with no link to the crossing car. Cases found there would not show the effect of the ego's speed or the crossing car's timing, so these four are left out. The 6 remaining scenes passed at the default settings. Several came close to the crossing car: 054b5901 at 0.26 m and 13a9767e at 0.12 m, so they are good candidates. 0d76134f is weak: the ego drove only 7 m there, but it is kept so that 5 different scenes stay reachable. Other actors follow their recorded tracks, as in Euro NCAP tests. The retime class is automobile. retime_tracks shifts only the crossing car in each scene, so the other recorded cars stay as recorded. The budget is 49 runs (7 rounds of 7), close to the ~50 asked for. The goal is the top 5 settings, one per scene, whose failure rate is surely above 0.5. Confirming one setting takes about 5 runs, almost all failures, so confirming 5 takes about 25 runs. That leaves about 24 for exploration, so the goal may not be met in full. If it is not, the study reports the strongest cases found and their near misses. What the plan cannot answer: - All 6 scenes are recorded as going straight ('turn: none'). None has both a recorded crossing car and a turn while also passing at the default settings, so turning across traffic is not covered. - Above ×1.0, the ego starts behind its recorded position and is off the recording for the first second or two of the warm-up. - The crossing car keeps its recorded speed, as the brief asked. Only its timing changes. - No planner delay or perception error is applied.
