SimGate study · rules

At intersections with a recorded crossing car, which combinations of the ego's speed at hand-off and the crossing car's arrival time make VaVAM with the linear MPC collide or leave the road? The goal is the 5 most challenging cases, each on a different scene, confirmed by repeats.

49 simulated drives on 6 scene(s) rebuilt from real recordings, varying ego speed, actor time shift.

What the words on this page mean
  • Run: one simulated drive of a scene by the driving policy under test.
  • Scene: a few seconds of a real recorded drive, rebuilt so the policy can drive it again; named by the first characters of its id.
  • Test car (the "ego"): the car the policy drives. The other road users replay what they did in the recording.
  • Recorded drive: the path the human driver took in the recording.
  • Kept: the run passed every automatic check that the simulator ran it as asked. Only kept runs count as evidence.
  • Failed: the test car hit something with its front or side, or left the road.
  • Setting: one scene at one knob value, numbered S1, S2, … so the findings can cite it.
  • 90% range: the failure rates that fit what was seen, at 90% confidence. Few runs give a wide range.

1. The question

At intersections with a recorded crossing car, which combinations of the ego's speed at hand-off and the crossing car's arrival time make VaVAM with the linear MPC collide or leave the road? The goal is the 5 most challenging cases, each on a different scene, confirmed by repeats.

What was varied: ego speed: speed of the test car when the driving policy takes over, relative to its recording at that place; actor time shift: seconds by which the retimed road user moves later than recorded (negative: earlier).

The researcher's brief, as written

# Intersection: ego speed against the crossing car's timing ## Question In scenes where the ego crosses or turns at an intersection with crossing traffic, which combinations of the ego's speed when the driving policy takes over and the crossing car's arrival time make the policy collide or leave the road? Find the 5 most challenging cases, each on a different scene, confirmed by repeats. ## Scope Scenes tagged intersection with a recorded crossing vehicle. Vary the ego's speed at hand-off (0.6x to 1.4x its recorded speed) and retime the crossing car (class automobile): arrive up to 2 s earlier or later, at its recorded speed. Replay the other traffic as recorded, as in Euro NCAP crossing tests, where the test vehicle's speed and the target's timing are the parameters. No delay or perception error. ## Budget About 50 runs. ## What counts A front or side collision, or leaving the road. Near misses rank next.

2. The answer

The study met only part of the goal: 2 of the 5 cases were confirmed, so it does not give a full top 5. The two cases are scene 02eadd92 at the recorded speed and timing (S2) and scene 054b5901 at 1.2× speed and the recorded timing (S6). Every repeat at each failed, but with only a few repeats per setting the ranges show only that the failure rate is above about one half. Neither case shows that the crossing car's timing matters. In both scenes the policy ignored the RIGHT command and drifted left. On 02eadd92 it rear-ended the crossing bus. On 054b5901 it ran onto the median with no car nearby. Every failure is flagged as a possible artifact because the ego ended more than 3.5 m from the recorded path. The video and log review shows that this gap comes from the policy's own steering, not from rendering.

Goal, checked by code (not by the model): 2 of 5 challenging settings confirmed, after 7 rounds.

Still open

  • The crossing car's timing was barely explored: only shifts from −1 s to +0.5 s were run, never the ±2 s range the brief asked for, so the study cannot say how arrival time affects failures. On 02eadd92, shifting bus 127 by −2 s to +2 s at 1.0× would show whether that crash depends on the bus's timing.
  • Three cases still need repeats before they count: 02eadd92 at 0.8× (S1) and 054b5901 at 1.2× with −0.5 s (S5) and at 1.4× (S7).
  • No third, fourth or fifth case on another scene was found. The best candidates are 13a9767e at 1.2–1.4× with the crossing car shifted ±1–2 s (it has the closest near misses), then 118a3400 and 1a7b81b8 above 1.0×, where no runs were made.
  • The 054b5901 failures depend on speed but do not involve the crossing car. Finding a case there that does involve the crossing car would need time shifts that bring car 93 into the ego's path at 1.0–1.2×.
  • Turning across traffic is not covered: all six scenes are recorded as going straight.
  • The policy ignoring the RIGHT command was behind every failure. It is unclear whether this is a general weakness in following route commands rather than a reaction to the crossing car. Running a scene without the crossing car would separate the two.

7. Where this came from

The plan was written by claude-opus-5-5 from the brief and checked by code against the knob catalog and the run budget. The runs were chosen in 7 rounds by rules; code rejected any choice outside the plan before it ran. Failures were explained by claude-opus-5-5. The answer and findings were written by claude-opus-5-5.

Runs: 49 in the study, 49 kept, 0 not kept; 9 of the kept runs failed.

How the numbers were computed: a run counts only if it passed every automatic check of the simulator (it is kept). A run failed if the test car hit something with its front or side, or left the road. For each setting, code counts the kept runs that failed and gives the 90% Wilson range of the failure rate. A failure is flagged as possibly the simulator's when the test car was more than 3.5 m (about a lane) from the recorded drive, or a camera frame was black, because there the rebuilt scene is drawn less reliably. The model wrote the words; every count and range on this page is computed from the run records, not written by the model.

Why the plan looks the way it does

Ten scenes are tagged intersection and have a recorded crossing vehicle. Four of them already failed at the default speed and timing: 048b974e, 05a1d902, 0e02fb8c and 0f1f60d5. Three of those four failures had a closest approach above 1 m, so they were probably road departures with no link to the crossing car. Cases found there would not show the effect of the ego's speed or the crossing car's timing, so these four are left out. The 6 remaining scenes passed at the default settings. Several came close to the crossing car: 054b5901 at 0.26 m and 13a9767e at 0.12 m, so they are good candidates. 0d76134f is weak: the ego drove only 7 m there, but it is kept so that 5 different scenes stay reachable. Other actors follow their recorded tracks, as in Euro NCAP tests.
