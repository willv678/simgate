SimGate study · pilot_o1

How does the policy's failure rate on each candidate scene change with planner delay in microseconds (camera frame to the controller receiving the plan made from it)? Find the fragile scenes and roughly where each breaks.

15 simulated drives on 4 scene(s) rebuilt from real recordings, varying planner delay.

What the words on this page mean
  • Run: one simulated drive of a scene by the driving policy under test.
  • Scene: a few seconds of a real recorded drive, rebuilt so the policy can drive it again; named by the first characters of its id.
  • Test car (the "ego"): the car the policy drives. The other road users replay what they did in the recording.
  • Recorded drive: the path the human driver took in the recording.
  • Kept: the run passed every automatic check that the simulator ran it as asked. Only kept runs count as evidence.
  • Failed: the test car hit something with its front or side, or left the road.
  • Setting: one scene at one knob value, numbered S1, S2, … so the findings can cite it.
  • 90% range: the failure rates that fit what was seen, at 90% confidence. Few runs give a wide range.

1. The question

How does the policy's failure rate on each candidate scene change with planner delay in microseconds (camera frame to the controller receiving the plan made from it)? Find the fragile scenes and roughly where each breaks.

What was varied: planner delay: time from a camera frame to the car's controller receiving the plan made from it.

The researcher's brief, as written

(No brief: the 6 Oct pilot o1 was started by run_pilot.sh with this fixed objective, 3 rounds of 5 runs, Claude proposing.) 2. The answer

Only one scene, 02eadd92, looks sensitive to planner delay. It passed every run at 0 and 100 ms and failed every run from 150 ms up. It most likely breaks somewhere between 100 and 150 ms, but each setting has only one or two runs, so this is a strong hint, not a measured rate. Two other scenes fail even with no delay (01d503d4 and 026d6a39), so delay doesn't explain them. A third, 023b7fcc, failed once at 300 ms but passed at 400 ms, so it has no clear breaking point. Four of the eight candidate scenes were never run.

No goal that code can check was set, so the study ran its whole budget.

Still open

  • Scenes 0245ff75, 02e075b9, 032b6f21 and 04394343 were never run, so whether they are fragile is unknown. Sweeping each from 0 to 400 ms would settle it.
  • Exactly where 02eadd92 breaks: repeated runs (about 5 per setting) at 100, 125 and 150 ms would narrow the threshold and confirm that the scene passes at 0 and 100 ms.
  • Whether delay affects 01d503d4 at all: the scene fails at 0 ms, and most of its failures are cut-ins by actor 27 where the policy's fault is unclear. Repeated runs at 0 ms and at higher delays, plus checking how the replayed cut-in plays out, would show whether the scene is a usable test.
  • Whether 023b7fcc's single failure at 300 ms is noise or a real effect of delay: repeated runs at 200, 300 and 400 ms would tell.
  • 026d6a39 was run only at 0 ms. More runs there, and at higher delays, would show whether it always leaves the road and whether the flagged camera smearing plays any part.

5. Why the runs failed

For each failed run, Claude looked at the camera frames just before and at the failure, and at the speed and the gap to the car ahead, and named a cause from a fixed list. The picture is the front camera at the failure moment.

Causes: did not brake enough for the car ahead: 4; another road user hit the test car: 2; steered into another road user: 2; left the road: 1; other: 1.

7. Where this came from

The plan was fixed by a script, not written by a model. The runs were chosen in 3 rounds by llm (claude-opus-5-5); code rejected any choice outside the plan before it ran. Failures were explained by claude-opus-5-5. The answer and findings were written by claude-opus-5-5.

Runs: 15 in the study, 15 kept, 0 not kept; 10 of the kept runs failed.

How the numbers were computed: a run counts only if it passed every automatic check of the simulator (it is kept). A run failed if the test car hit something with its front or side, or left the road. For each setting, code counts the kept runs that failed and gives the 90% Wilson range of the failure rate. A failure is flagged as possibly the simulator's when the test car was more than 3.5 m (about a lane) from the recorded drive, or a camera frame was black, because there the rebuilt scene is drawn less reliably. The model wrote the words; every count and range on this page is computed from the run records, not written by the model.
