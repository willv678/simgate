SimGate study · optuna

Which pedestrian step-out timings (±2 s) and walking speeds (0.5x to 2x) make VaVAM hit the pedestrian or leave the road in pedestrian-crossing scenes, with the other traffic replayed as recorded? Find the 5 hardest cases, each on a different scene.

49 simulated drives on 6 scene(s) rebuilt from real recordings, varying actor time shift, actor speed.

What the words on this page mean
  • Run: one simulated drive of a scene by the driving policy under test.
  • Scene: a few seconds of a real recorded drive, rebuilt so the policy can drive it again; named by the first characters of its id.
  • Test car (the "ego"): the car the policy drives. The other road users replay what they did in the recording.
  • Recorded drive: the path the human driver took in the recording.
  • Kept: the run passed every automatic check that the simulator ran it as asked. Only kept runs count as evidence.
  • Failed: the test car hit something with its front or side, or left the road.
  • Setting: one scene at one knob value, numbered S1, S2, … so the findings can cite it.
  • 90% range: the failure rates that fit what was seen, at 90% confidence. Few runs give a wide range.

1. The question

Which pedestrian step-out timings (±2 s) and walking speeds (0.5x to 2x) make VaVAM hit the pedestrian or leave the road in pedestrian-crossing scenes, with the other traffic replayed as recorded? Find the 5 hardest cases, each on a different scene.

What was varied: actor time shift: seconds by which the retimed road user moves later than recorded (negative: earlier); actor speed: speed of the retimed road user relative to its recording.

The researcher's brief, as written

# Pedestrian crossing: the most challenging timings ## Question In scenes where a pedestrian crosses or walks near the ego's path, which pedestrian timings and speeds make the driving policy hit the pedestrian or leave the road to avoid them? Find the 5 most challenging cases, each on a different scene, confirmed by repeats. ## Scope Scenes tagged pedestrian_crossing. Retime the recorded pedestrians (class person): step out up to 2 s earlier or later, walk 0.5x to 2x their recorded speed. Replay the other traffic as recorded so the scenario is scripted, as in Euro NCAP pedestrian tests. No delay or perception error. ## Budget About 50 runs. ## What counts A front or side collision, or leaving the road. Near misses rank next.

2. The answer

The study confirmed one of the five hard cases it was asked to find, so the goal was not met. On scene 13a9767e, a pedestrian who steps out 1.0 s later and walks at 0.5x their recorded speed (S30) made VaVAM fail every time it was run. In each of those runs the ego hit the crossing pedestrian with its front while turning right, because it slowed only gently and never stopped. This case only just clears the threshold, and one of its failures is flagged as a possible simulator artifact (the ego was more than 3.5 m from the recorded path). If that run is discounted, the case is not confirmed. No timing or speed tried on the other five scenes caused a failure, but almost all of those settings ran only once, so they are hints, not evidence that those scenes are robust.

Goal, checked by code (not by the model): 1 of 5 challenging settings confirmed, after 7 rounds.

Still open

  • Whether the confirmed case holds up without the run flagged as a possible artifact. More repeats at 13a9767e with a 1.0 s delay and 0.5x walk (S30) would show whether failure stays above half once the lateral offset is ruled out as a rendering effect.
  • Whether the near misses are real failures. Repeats are needed at 13a9767e with a 1.0 s delay and 0.75x walk (S31), at 0e002edd with a 2.0 s delay and 1.5x walk (S17), and at 095cf563 with a 1.0 s delay and 0.5x walk (S4).
  • Whether 0e002edd, 095cf563, 18f8dbd6 and 0b10bce8 are robust or just under-sampled. Almost every setting on them ran once. A few repeats at each scene's highest-criticality setting would settle this.
  • Whether retiming actually works on 18f8dbd6, 0b10bce8 and 12a09194. Their criticality did not change with timing. Their logs should be checked to confirm that the retimed pedestrian (tracks 97, 32 and 123) really moved, and to see whether 0b10bce8's ego is nearly stopped.
  • With only six candidate scenes, five confirmed cases on different scenes may not be reachable. Testing the six pedestrian_crossing scenes the plan left out, under replay traffic, would widen the pool. Their baselines were measured with CATK traffic, not replay, so they first need a check at zero shift and 1.0x speed.

7. Where this came from

The plan was written by claude-opus-5-5 from the brief and checked by code against the knob catalog and the run budget. The runs were chosen in 7 rounds by optuna; code rejected any choice outside the plan before it ran. Failures were explained by claude-opus-5-5. The answer and findings were written by claude-opus-5-5.

Runs: 49 in the study, 49 kept, 0 not kept; 3 of the kept runs failed.

How the numbers were computed: a run counts only if it passed every automatic check of the simulator (it is kept). A run failed if the test car hit something with its front or side, or left the road. For each setting, code counts the kept runs that failed and gives the 90% Wilson range of the failure rate. A failure is flagged as possibly the simulator's when the test car was more than 3.5 m (about a lane) from the recorded drive, or a camera frame was black, because there the rebuilt scene is drawn less reliably. The model wrote the words; every count and range on this page is computed from the run records, not written by the model.

Why the plan looks the way it does

Scope: the brief asks for scenes tagged pedestrian_crossing, with recorded pedestrians retimed (class person), other traffic on replay (as in Euro NCAP-style scripted tests), and no delay or perception error.
