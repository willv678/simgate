With the warm-up seam fix applied, does every ego speed on 07981e6a pass the physics gate?

5 simulated drives on 1 scene(s) rebuilt from real recordings, varying ego speed.

What the words on this page mean
  • Run: one simulated drive of a scene by the driving policy under test.
  • Scene: a few seconds of a real recorded drive, rebuilt so the policy can drive it again; named by the first characters of its id.
  • Test car (the "ego"): the car the policy drives. The other road users replay what they did in the recording.
  • Recorded drive: the path the human driver took in the recording.
  • Kept: the run passed every automatic check that the simulator ran it as asked. Only kept runs count as evidence.
  • Failed: the test car hit something with its front or side, or left the road.
  • Setting: one scene at one knob value, numbered S1, S2, … so the findings can cite it.
  • 90% range: the failure rates that fit what was seen, at 90% confidence. Few runs give a wide range.

1. The question

With the warm-up seam fix applied, does every ego speed on 07981e6a pass the physics gate?

What was varied: ego speed: speed of the test car when the driving policy takes over, relative to its recording at that place.

The researcher's brief, as written

# Warm-up seam check One run per ego_speed_scale value on scene 07981e6a, where the warm-up's extrapolation before the recording once met the recording at 10.7 m/s^2 (1.2x, FACTS problem 24) and the physics bound halted both runs. Checks the seam fix in a simulation: every run should pass the physics gate (written by hand; see plan.json).

2. The answer

Yes. Every ego speed from 0.6x to 1.4x on 07981e6a passed the physics gate. That includes 1.2x, the setting that halted before. Every run passed every validity check, and no run was dropped, so the warm-up seam fix held in simulation. This rests on one run per speed. It shows the seam stays within the physics bound for this scene, but it doesn't measure how often a later run might fail the gate. Apart from the gate, every run crashed. Triage blames the policy in each case, not the simulator: after handoff the ego drifted left into the parked cars even though the route command was RIGHT.

No goal that code can check was set, so the study ran its whole budget.

Still open

  • How reliable the fix is: one run per speed shows the gate can pass at each speed, but not how often it does. Repeating several runs at 1.2x and its neighbours (1.0x, 1.4x) would show whether the seam ever comes near the physics bound again.
  • Why the policy drifts left on 07981e6a at every speed when told to go RIGHT: more runs per speed, or a different controller on the same scene, would show whether this crash happens every time and whether it comes from this policy alone.

5. Why the runs failed

For each failed run, Claude looked at the camera frames just before and at the failure, and at the speed and the gap to the car ahead, and named a cause from a fixed list. The picture is the front camera at the failure moment.

Causes: left the road: 4; steered into another road user: 1.

7. Where this came from

The plan was fixed by a script, not written by a model. The runs were chosen in 1 rounds by grid; code rejected any choice outside the plan before it ran. Failures were explained by claude-opus-5-5. The answer and findings were written by claude-opus-5-5.

Runs: 5 in the study, 5 kept, 0 not kept; 5 of the kept runs failed.

How the numbers were computed: a run counts only if it passed every automatic check of the simulator (it is kept). A run failed if the test car hit something with its front or side, or left the road. For each setting, code counts the kept runs that failed and gives the 90% Wilson range of the failure rate. A failure is flagged as possibly the simulator's when the test car was more than 3.5 m (about a lane) from the recorded drive, or a camera frame was black, because there the rebuilt scene is drawn less reliably. The model wrote the words; every count and range on this page is computed from the run records, not written by the model.
