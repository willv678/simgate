# In pedestrian-crossing scenes, which combinations of ego speed at hand-off (0.6x–1.4x recorded) and pedestrian step-out time (±2 s) make the VaVAM policy with the linear MPC collide or leave the road? Find the 5 most challenging cases, each on a different scene, confirmed by repeats.

Study `random_confirm_r2`: 50 kept runs on 9 scenes, varying ego_speed_scale, actor_time_shift_s; 0 runs not kept by the gate. Counts are kept runs only.

**Goal (checked by code, not the model):** 3 of 5 challenging settings confirmed, after 5 rounds.

## Answer

The study did not meet its goal. It confirmed 3 of the 5 cases it was asked to find, each on a different scene: 0e002edd at 1.4x / −1.5 s (S19), 07981e6a at 0.6x / +1.5 s (S3) and 1bbe02fc at 0.8x / −2.0 s (S31). Each of these is backed by only three repeats, so the true failure rate could be anywhere from just above one half to always. None of the failures involved the pedestrian. The triage shows every one was the policy hitting a parked car, rear-ending or sideswiping another vehicle, or leaving the road, almost always after drifting left or ignoring a RIGHT route command. So the study shows where VaVAM at 2 Hz fails in these scenes, but not that the pedestrian's step-out time causes it. Ego speed seems to matter on two scenes (0e002edd and 18d28973 failed only at 1.4x), but that rests on single runs at the lower speeds. Two caveats: two of S19's failures had the ego far off the recorded path, so the simulator may partly explain them; and two of S31's three failures were marginal off-road flags at the curb edge.

## Open

- No scene was run at the nominal setting (1.0x, 0 s), so the study cannot say whether the confirmed cases need the speed and timing change or would fail anyway. 07981e6a and 1bbe02fc look like they would. Three to five nominal runs on each confirmed scene would settle this.
- The 4th and 5th cases are not confirmed. Two or three more repeats each at 18d28973 1.4x / +1.0 s (S30), 0d4893f5 1.4x / +1.5 s (S15) and 048b974e 1.2x / −1.0 s (S1) would likely fill both slots.
- Whether pedestrian timing has any effect is unresolved, because no failure involved the pedestrian and the failure check cannot tell which actor was hit. On a clean scene such as 0e002edd at 1.4x, sweeping the shift from −2 s to +2 s with repeats, and logging the closest approach to the pedestrian specifically, would separate the timing effect from the speed effect.
- The speed effect on 0e002edd and 18d28973 rests on single non-failing runs at lower speeds. Repeats at 1.0x and 1.2x with the same shift would confirm or rule it out.
- The S31 off-road failures under 1 m from the human path, and the possible artifacts in S19 and S33, need a check of the map edge and the rendering before these are counted as real policy failures.
- This report cannot compare 2 Hz with the 10 Hz study (pedestrian_ego_speed). Doing so means reading that study's results at the same settings, especially whether the left drift into parked cars on 07981e6a also shows up at 10 Hz.

## Provenance

Plan: `plan.json` (rationale: Copied from studies/pedestrian_ego_speed/plan.json (10 Hz) with the policy planning every 500 ms, so the method comparison can be repeated at 2 Hz. Scenes: these are the 10 scenes tagged pedestrian_crossing that have a recorded pedestrian key actor. Each scene's pedestrian is set in retime_tracks, so actor_time_shift_s moves only that person. actor_speed_scale stays at 1.0, so the pedestrian walks at the recorded speed. Traffic is replay, which keeps the scenario scripted as in Euro NCAP pedestrian tests. There is no delay, bias or noise.

Two other tagged scenes are left out: 095cf563 and 13a9767e. Neither has a pedestrian key actor, so a person-class retime there might hit no actor or the wrong one.

Budget: 5 rounds of 10 runs gives 50 runs, as the brief asks. The goal is top_k with k=5, high_min=0.5 and distinct scenes. With 90% ranges, each confirmed case needs about 4–5 failures out of 5 repeats, so roughly 25 runs go to confirmation and about 25 to searching the 5×9 grid. That is tight, and with partial results the study may confirm fewer than 5 cases.
