# In pedestrian-crossing scenes, which combinations of ego speed at hand-off (0.6x–1.4x recorded) and pedestrian step-out time (±2 s) make the VaVAM policy with the linear MPC collide or leave the road? Find the 5 most challenging cases, each on a different scene, confirmed by repeats.

Study `llm_r3`: 49 kept runs on 9 scenes, varying ego_speed_scale, actor_time_shift_s; 2 runs not kept by the gate. Counts are kept runs only.

**Goal (checked by code, not the model):** 4 of 5 challenging settings confirmed, after 5 rounds.

## Answer

The study confirmed 4 of the 5 cases it was asked for, so the goal was not met. The 4 cases are 18f8dbd6 at 1.4x / −1 s (S18), 07981e6a at 1.4x / −1 s (S3), 0e002edd at 1.4x / −1 s (S11) and 1bbe02fc at 1.2x / −1 s (S20). None of the triaged failures involved the pedestrian. The policy hit other cars or parked cars after drifting left or not braking for a slow lead car, or it was flagged offroad. What drives the failures is high hand-off speed, often with a RIGHT route command the policy did not follow, not the pedestrian's timing. The 1bbe02fc case may say more about the map than the policy: its offroad flags are marginal (under 1 m off the human path), it also failed in its single run at 1.0x (a hint, not a rate), and every S11 failure is marked as a possible simulator artifact, although triage blames the policy in each.

## Open

- No scene was run at the nominal 1.0x / 0 s, so the study cannot show that the confirmed failures need the speed–timing change rather than happening anyway. Repeats at 1.0x / 0 s on 18f8dbd6, 07981e6a, 0e002edd and 1bbe02fc would settle this.
- The pedestrian's timing has no shown effect, and no failure involved the pedestrian. Almost all runs used shifts of −2 s to −0.5 s at 1.2x to 1.4x. Ego speeds of 0.6x to 0.8x and positive shifts were barely or never tried, so pedestrian-strike cases were not searched. Sweeping the shift from −2 s to +2 s at a fixed 1.4x on one confirmed scene would show whether timing matters.
- Fifth case: more repeats at 18d28973 1.4x / −1.5 s or 0d4893f5 1.4x / −1 s could narrow the ranges. Both currently sit around 0.5, so they may not be confirmed even then.
- Whether the 1bbe02fc offroad flags are real departures or map drivable-area artifacts. Checking the map boundary at that stop line, plus more runs at 1.0x, would settle it.
- Whether the S11 failures, all flagged as possible artifacts, are reproducible after a rendering check.
- The comparison with the 10 Hz study pedestrian_ego_speed needs that study's results next to these. This report does not include them.

## Provenance

Plan: `plan.json` (rationale: Copied from studies/pedestrian_ego_speed/plan.json (10 Hz) with the policy planning every 500 ms, so the method comparison can be repeated at 2 Hz. Scenes: these are the 10 scenes tagged pedestrian_crossing that have a recorded pedestrian key actor. Each scene's pedestrian is set in retime_tracks, so actor_time_shift_s moves only that person. actor_speed_scale stays at 1.0, so the pedestrian walks at the recorded speed. Traffic is replay, which keeps the scenario scripted as in Euro NCAP pedestrian tests. There is no delay, bias or noise.

Two other tagged scenes are left out: 095cf563 and 13a9767e. Neither has a pedestrian key actor, so a person-class retime there might hit no actor or the wrong one.

Budget: 5 rounds of 10 runs gives 50 runs, as the brief asks. The goal is top_k with k=5, high_min=0.5 and distinct scenes. With 90% ranges, each confirmed case needs about 4–5 failures out of 5 repeats, so roughly 25 runs go to confirmation and about 25 to searching the 5×9 grid. That is tight, and with partial results the study may confirm fewer than 5 cases.

Caveats:
- 6 of the scenes (048b974e, 07981e6a, 0caa8f1a, 0d4893f5, 18d28973, 1bbe02fc) failed at 0 delay in an earlier CATK run. That run used reactive traffic, not replay, so it may not carry over. - 0e002edd, 18f8dbd6, 12a09194 and 0b10bce8 are the cleaner tests. In 0b10bce8 the ego barely moved (8.9 m), and in 12a09194 the pedestrian stayed far away (11.5 m).
