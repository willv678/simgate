# In pedestrian-crossing scenes, which combinations of ego speed at hand-off (0.6x–1.4x recorded) and pedestrian step-out time (±2 s) make the VaVAM policy with the linear MPC collide or leave the road? Find the 5 most challenging cases, each on a different scene, confirmed by repeats.

Study `optuna_confirm_r3`: 49 kept runs on 10 scenes, varying ego_speed_scale, actor_time_shift_s; 2 runs not kept by the gate. Counts are kept runs only.

**Goal (checked by code, not the model):** 4 of 5 challenging settings confirmed, after 5 rounds.

## Answer

The goal was not met. Repeats confirmed four challenging cases on four different scenes, one short of five: 048b974e at 0.6x / +1.0 s (S1), 0d4893f5 at 1.2x / −2.0 s (S13), 1bbe02fc at 0.6x / −1.5 s (S24) and 07981e6a at 1.4x / −1.5 s (S7). Every repeat at each of these failed, but with only a few repeats the 90% ranges still reach down to about one half. None of these failures, and none of the failures anywhere in the study, involved the pedestrian. The triage shows the ego drifting or turning left against a RIGHT route command and hitting parked cars, a vehicle in the next lane or an oncoming van, or being flagged off-road. So the speed and timing knobs did not produce pedestrian collisions. On 07981e6a and 1bbe02fc, every setting tried failed, including near-nominal ones, which points to the policy's left drift in those scenes rather than to the speed–timing combination. On 1bbe02fc, several of the off-road flags were marginal and may come from the drivable-area map rather than a real departure.
