SimGate study · hybrid

Does the tuned linear MPC (feasible_best: Riccati terminal cost, stiffer position tracking) fail less often than the default linear MPC when the key pedestrian steps out up to 2 s early and walks 1x to 2x their recorded speed?

21 simulated drives on 4 scene(s) rebuilt from real recordings, varying actor time shift, actor speed, controller.

What the words on this page mean
  • Run: one simulated drive of a scene by the driving policy under test.
  • Scene: a few seconds of a real recorded drive, rebuilt so the policy can drive it again; named by the first characters of its id.
  • Test car (the "ego"): the car the policy drives. The other road users replay what they did in the recording.
  • Recorded drive: the path the human driver took in the recording.
  • Kept: the run passed every automatic check that the simulator ran it as asked. Only kept runs count as evidence.
  • Failed: the test car hit something with its front or side, or left the road.
  • Setting: one scene at one knob value, numbered S1, S2, … so the findings can cite it.
  • 90% range: the failure rates that fit what was seen, at 90% confidence. Few runs give a wide range.

1. The question

Does the tuned linear MPC (feasible_best: Riccati terminal cost, stiffer position tracking) fail less often than the default linear MPC when the key pedestrian steps out up to 2 s early and walks 1x to 2x their recorded speed?

What was varied: actor time shift: seconds by which the retimed road user moves later than recorded (negative: earlier); actor speed: speed of the retimed road user relative to its recording; controller: the controller that steers and brakes the car along the policy's plan: the system the A/B study compares.

The researcher's brief, as written

# Default or tuned MPC: which is safer when a pedestrian steps out early? ## Question Is the tuned linear MPC (feasible_best: a Riccati terminal cost and stiffer position tracking) safer than the default linear MPC when pedestrians step out early? The driving policy and everything else stay the same; only the controller's tuning changes. Compare the default linear MPC (a) with feasible_best (b). ## Scope Scenes tagged pedestrian_crossing with a pedestrian close to the ego's path. Retime only that key pedestrian: step out up to 2 s earlier than recorded, walking 1x to 2x their recorded speed. Replay the other traffic as recorded so both controllers face the same scripted scenario. No delay or perception error. Leave out scenes that already fail without any change. ## Budget About 50 runs, counting each controller's run separately. ## What counts A front or side collision, or leaving the road. The answer is which controller fails less over all the timings tried, or that the runs show no difference.

2. The answer

No. The tuned controller (feasible_best) was not safer: the default linear MPC failed less, and the pooled 90% ranges of the two controllers separate. feasible_best failed in every pair, on all four scenes, and linear failed in none. The sign test over pairs where only one controller failed points the same way. Two things limit this. First, none of feasible_best's failures involved the pedestrian. On a RIGHT command it turned too sharply into parked cars or steered left off the road. It did this even at the recorded timing and speed, so the result shows that feasible_best is broken in these scenes, not that it handles early pedestrians worse. Second, the study stopped after two rounds and only tried step-outs of 0 to 0.5 s early at 1.0x to 1.5x speed. Most settings have one pair each, so how linear handles a pedestrian 1 to 2 s early is still untested.

Goal, checked by code (not by the model): linear fails less than feasible_best: the pooled 90% ranges separate, after 2 rounds.

  • linear: 0 of 10 paired runs failed, failure rate 0%–21% (90% range)
  • feasible_best: 10 of 10 paired runs failed, failure rate 79%–100% (90% range)

Sign test over the pairs where only one controller failed: 0 against 10, two-sided p = 0.002.

Still open

  • How linear handles a pedestrian stepping out 1 to 2 s early, or walking faster than 1.5x, is untested: no setting went past -0.5 s or 1.5x. Linear-only runs on 0e002edd and 18f8dbd6 at shifts of -1.0, -1.5 and -2.0 s and at 1.5x to 2.0x speed would settle whether linear holds up there.
  • Whether feasible_best fails on these scenes with no retiming at all, which would confirm that its failures come from the tuning rather than the pedestrian. Unmodified baseline runs of feasible_best on all four scenes, repeated a few times, would settle it.
  • Whether the pedestrian scenario can tell the controllers apart at all. That needs a controller that can complete the right turn (fix feasible_best's turning first), then paired runs on the same early timings.
  • Most settings have a single pair, so the per-setting results are hints. The separation rests on pooling across settings, and the study stopped after 2 of the 5 planned rounds.
  • One linear run at S4 (hybrid_005_linear) was not kept and had to be re-run. The replacement (hybrid_005_linear_a2) is the one counted.

7. Where this came from

The plan was written by claude-opus-5-5 from the brief and checked by code against the knob catalog and the run budget. The runs were chosen in 2 rounds by hybrid (claude-opus-5-5); code rejected any choice outside the plan before it ran. Failures were explained by claude-opus-5-5. The answer and findings were written by claude-opus-5-5.

Runs: 21 in the study, 20 kept, 1 not kept; 10 of the kept runs failed.

How the numbers were computed: a run counts only if it passed every automatic check of the simulator (it is kept). A run failed if the test car hit something with its front or side, or left the road. For each setting, code counts the kept runs that failed and gives the 90% Wilson range of the failure rate. A failure is flagged as possibly the simulator's when the test car was more than 3.5 m (about a lane) from the recorded drive, or a camera frame was black, because there the rebuilt scene is drawn less reliably. The model wrote the words; every count and range on this page is computed from the run records, not written by the model.

Why the plan looks the way it does

This is an A/B study of linear (a) against feasible_best (b). Each setting runs once on each controller with the same scene and knob values, so only the tuning differs. Traffic is replayed so both controllers face the same scripted scenario. Only the key pedestrian is retimed: retime_class is person, and retime_tracks names one pedestrian per scene. The two varied knobs are actor_time_shift_s and actor_speed_scale. The brief's range is -2.0 to 0.0 s and 1.0x to 2.0x. The objective holds the proposer to that range, because the knobs' legal values go beyond it. Delay, lateral bias and waypoint noise stay at their defaults of 0, so there is no delay and no perception error. Scenes: there are 12 scenes tagged pedestrian_crossing. Six already failed at 0 delay and are left out, as the brief asks: 048b974e, 07981e6a, 0caa8f1a, 0d4893f5, 18d28973 and 1bbe02fc. Two others list no pedestrian key actor, so there is no key pedestrian to retime: 095cf563 and 13a9767e. That leaves 4 scenes: - 0e002edd (pedestrian 12): closest approach 0.15 m. - 18f8dbd6 (pedestrian 97): closest approach 0.9 m. - 12a09194 (pedestrian 123): closest approach to any actor was 11.5 m, so its pedestrian may never reach the ego's path even when retimed. - 0b10bce8 (pedestrian 32): in the earlier run the ego drove only 8.9 m (progress 0.11), so it may never reach the crossing. The last two scenes may never fail on either controller. Those runs say nothing about which controller is safer, and the proposer is told to move away from such settings. Budget: 5 rounds × 5 settings × 2 controllers = 50 runs, the brief's figure and within the cap of 60. The goal is compare. The study stops early if the 90% ranges of the two pooled failure rates separate. Otherwise it reports that no difference was shown, along with the exact sign test over pairs where only one controller failed. Limits: - With 25 pairs and few scenes, only a large difference between the controllers is likely to show up. - The result applies only to these 4 scenes and these pedestrians. - A single earlier run is a weak basis for calling a scene "not failing at baseline": one sample can pass once and fail the next time. - Retiming a pedestrian in replay cannot make the pedestrian react to the ego.
