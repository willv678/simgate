# In cut-in and lead-vehicle scenes that pass as recorded, which combinations of the car's timing, the car's speed and the ego's speed at hand-off make the policy collide or leave the road? The aim is the 5 most challenging cases, each on a different scene, confirmed by repeats.

Study `hybrid_optuna_r2`: 60 kept runs on 10 scenes, varying actor_time_shift_s, actor_speed_scale, ego_speed_scale; 1 runs not kept by the gate. Counts are kept runs only.

**Goal (checked by code, not the model):** 4 of 5 challenging settings confirmed, after 6 rounds.

## Answer

The goal was not met: the study confirmed four of the five challenging settings, each on a different scene (S1, S2, S18, S19). Only three of them are failures of the policy itself. At S1, S2 and S18 the policy steered left out of its lane, against its route command, and sideswiped a car in the next lane. At S19 the failures are a replayed car cutting into an ego that was running about 20 m behind its recorded position, so they are a gap in replay-only traffic, not a policy fault. S2 and S18 rest on few repeats and sit just above the 0.5 bar. No run left the road and none rear-ended a lead car. In every failure the car hit was not the retimed car, so the ego's speed and timing against the rest of the traffic seem to matter more than the retimed car's own timing or speed.
