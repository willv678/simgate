SimGate study · llm_r4

In lead-vehicle scenes, which timing shifts and speed scalings of the recorded lead car (with up to 200 ms planner delay if needed) make VaVAM run into it? Find the 5 most challenging cases, each on a different scene, confirmed by repeats.

29 simulated drives on 8 scene(s) rebuilt from real recordings, varying actor time shift, actor speed, planner delay.

What the words on this page mean
  • Run: one simulated drive of a scene by the driving policy under test.
  • Scene: a few seconds of a real recorded drive, rebuilt so the policy can drive it again; named by the first characters of its id.
  • Test car (the "ego"): the car the policy drives. The other road users replay what they did in the recording.
  • Recorded drive: the path the human driver took in the recording.
  • Kept: the run passed every automatic check that the simulator ran it as asked. Only kept runs count as evidence.
  • Failed: the test car hit something with its front or side, or left the road.
  • Setting: one scene at one knob value, numbered S1, S2, … so the findings can cite it.
  • 90% range: the failure rates that fit what was seen, at 90% confidence. Few runs give a wide range.

1. The question

In lead-vehicle scenes, which timing shifts and speed scalings of the recorded lead car (with up to 200 ms planner delay if needed) make VaVAM run into it? Find the 5 most challenging cases, each on a different scene, confirmed by repeats.

What was varied: actor time shift: seconds by which the retimed road user moves later than recorded (negative: earlier); actor speed: speed of the retimed road user relative to its recording; planner delay: time from a camera frame to the car's controller receiving the plan made from it.

The researcher's brief, as written

# Lead vehicle braking: the most challenging cases ## Question In scenes with a car ahead in the ego's lane that slows or stops, which timings and speeds of the vehicles ahead make the policy rear-end them? Find the 5 most challenging cases, each on a different scene, confirmed by repeats. ## Scope Scenes tagged lead_vehicle. Retime the recorded cars (class automobile): up to 2 s earlier or later, 0.5x to 2x their recorded speed (slower means a harder stop for the ego). Replay traffic as recorded. Planner delay may be added up to 200 ms if the cases at zero delay are not challenging enough. ## Budget About 50 runs. ## What counts A front or side collision, or leaving the road. Near misses rank next.

2. The answer

The study's goal is met as counted: five settings on five different scenes failed in every repeat (S15, S1, S16, S17, S3). All five slow the lead car to 0.5x, four of them also shift it +1 s, and none needs planner delay. But only one of these is a real policy failure, and it is not a rear-end. The triage of the video and logs shows that every failure at S1, S3, S15 and S16 (and the single run at S4) happened while the ego was still replaying the recorded human drive. The slowed lead car's box was already overlapping the ego before the policy took control, and the failure was counted at the handoff moment. Those four are artifacts of retiming the lead car against a fixed logged ego path, not cases where VaVAM rear-ends a braking car. S17 (scene 19f339ba) is the only setting where the policy was at fault, in every run, and there it braked for no clear reason and steered left into a car in the next lane, with no lead car involved. So the study found no confirmed case of the policy rear-ending a slowing or stopping lead car.

Goal, checked by code (not by the model): 5 of 5 challenging settings confirmed, after 4 rounds.

Still open

  • Whether VaVAM rear-ends a braking lead car at all is unsettled. Every lead-car contact counted here happened before handoff. Re-run 0e899dd3, 023b7fcc, 12855a41 and 096988dd with milder retiming (for example 0.7x to 0.9x speed, or shifts that keep the gap open until after the 4.7 s handoff), so that any closing happens while the policy is in control.
  • The validity gate did not flag these pre-handoff collisions: possible_artifacts is 0 on every row. Failures that are already present at handoff should be filtered out before the goal counts them.
  • Is the S17 lane drift on 19f339ba caused by the retiming, or does the policy do it with the recorded timing too? Run the scene unretimed and at 0.5x speed with a 0 s shift.
  • 0cf6368d at +2 s and 0.5x speed (S9) was the closest policy-driven call. Repeat it, and try +2 s with 100 to 200 ms of delay, to see whether it tips into failure after handoff.
  • The study ran only lead-car speeds of 0.5x and shifts of 0 to +2 s. Negative shifts and speeds between 0.5x and 2x were not tested.

7. Where this came from

The plan was written by claude-opus-5-5 from the brief and checked by code against the knob catalog and the run budget. The runs were chosen in 4 rounds by llm (claude-opus-5-5); code rejected any choice outside the plan before it ran. Failures were explained by claude-opus-5-5. The answer and findings were written by claude-opus-5-5.

Runs: 29 in the study, 28 kept, 1 not kept; 17 of the kept runs failed.

How the numbers were computed: a run counts only if it passed every automatic check of the simulator (it is kept). A run failed if the test car hit something with its front or side, or left the road. For each setting, code counts the kept runs that failed and gives the 90% Wilson range of the failure rate. A failure is flagged as possibly the simulator's when the test car was more than 3.5 m (about a lane) from the recorded drive, or a camera frame was black, because there the rebuilt scene is drawn less reliably. The model wrote the words; every count and range on this page is computed from the run records, not written by the model.

Why the plan looks the way it does

Scene choice: all eight scenes are tagged lead_vehicle, have an identified lead car, did not fail in the earlier run at 0 delay, and the ego drove far enough (6 to 19 m/s, about 35 to 130 m) to actually close on the car ahead. Several already came very close (0dbb91dd 0.23 m, 19f339ba 0.03 m, 12855a41 0.08 m, 096988dd and 023b7fcc 0.0 m), so they are likely to tip into failure. I left out lead_vehicle scenes that already fail at baseline, because they cannot show that the retiming caused the failure. I also left out scenes where the ego barely moved (for example 0fa7060f, 15dd433e, 0ac02819, 10d64934) and tagged scenes with no identified lead car (166fe701, 1bccdc21, 0ce02937, 1a5da90a). Retiming: traffic is replay, as the brief asks, so the lead car follows its recorded path while being retimed. Only the lead car is retimed in each scene (via retime_tracks), not every car. That keeps the failures about the car ahead rather than about crossing traffic being shifted. 0cf6368d's lead car is also its cut-in car, so a failure there may be a cut-in rather than a pure braking case. Goal: top_k with k=5, one setting per scene, and each setting's failure rate surely above 0.5. This matches 'the 5 most challenging cases, each on a different scene, confirmed by repeats'.
