# In pedestrian-crossing scenes, which combinations of ego speed at hand-off (0.6x–1.4x recorded) and pedestrian step-out time (±2 s) make the VaVAM policy with the linear MPC collide or leave the road? Find the 5 most challenging cases, each on a different scene, confirmed by repeats.

Study `random_confirm`: 39 kept runs on 10 scenes, varying ego_speed_scale, actor_time_shift_s; 1 runs not kept by the gate. Counts are kept runs only.

**Goal (checked by code, not the model):** 5 of 5 challenging settings confirmed, after 4 rounds.

## Answer

The study confirmed five settings above the 0.5 bar, each on a different scene, which meets the goal: S1 (048b974e, 0.6x, −1.5 s), S6 (07981e6a, 1.4x, −0.5 s), S10 (0caa8f1a, 1.0x, +1.5 s), S14 (0d4893f5, 1.2x, +1.0 s) and S20 (18d28973, 0.8x, 0 s). Every repeat at these five failed, but with so few repeats the 90% ranges only show the failure rate is above one half. However, these settings don't answer the question that was asked. The triage of the videos and logs shows that no failure involved the pedestrian. In every case the policy ignored a RIGHT route command and drifted or turned left. It then hit a parked or oncoming car, a car in the next lane, or ran onto a median. These five scenes also failed at very different speeds and step-out times, so the failures come from the scene and the policy's route-following, not from the speed–timing combination. The study found no setting where speed and pedestrian timing together cause a pedestrian collision. S1, S10 and S20 were also flagged as possible simulator artifacts because the ego ended far from the recorded path, but the triage puts these on the policy: the drift started while the camera view was still clean.

The failure check also can't tell which actor was hit. A follow-up should score closest approach to the retimed pedestrian track specifically, and search the scenes without route-following failures (0e002edd, 18f8dbd6, 0b10bce8, 12a09194) more densely, especially at 1.2–1.4x with early step-outs.
- Nominal 1.0x / 0 s was never run on the five confirmed scenes. Running it a few times on each would show whether they fail regardless of the knobs, as the triage suggests.
- The confirmed cases rest on only a few repeats each. More repeats at S1, S6, S10, S14 and S20 would narrow their failure-rate ranges.
- S25 on 1bbe02fc is just below confirmation, and its off-road flags are marginal. Repeating it, plus a check of the road boundary at that stop line, would settle whether it is a real failure.
