# In pedestrian-crossing scenes, which combinations of ego speed at hand-off (0.6x–1.4x recorded) and pedestrian step-out time (±2 s) make the VaVAM policy with the linear MPC collide or leave the road? Find the 5 most challenging cases, each on a different scene, confirmed by repeats.

Study `random_confirm_r3`: 40 kept runs on 9 scenes, varying ego_speed_scale, actor_time_shift_s; 0 runs not kept by the gate. Counts are kept runs only.

**Goal (checked by code, not the model):** 5 of 5 challenging settings confirmed, after 4 rounds.

## Answer

The goal was met: five settings on five different scenes failed on every repeat. They are 048b974e at 0.8x / −1.0 s (S3), 07981e6a at 1.4x / +1.0 s (S7), 0caa8f1a at 0.8x / −1.0 s (S10), 0d4893f5 at 0.6x / +1.5 s (S12) and 18d28973 at 1.4x / +0.5 s (S17). Each rests on three repeats, so the 90% ranges only show a failure rate above one half. They do not pin down the rate. None of these failures involved the pedestrian. The triage shows the same policy error every time: the route command said turn right, but VaVAM went left or took the turn badly. It then left the road or hit a parked or oncoming car. On the scenes searched more widely, failures occurred at nearly every speed and timing tried, so the study does not show that any speed and step-out combination causes these failures. The policy's route-following error causes them, and the pedestrian retiming looks irrelevant. Four of the five confirmed settings have every failure flagged as a possible simulator artifact (S3, S10, S12, S17), because the ego ended up more than 3.5 m from the recorded path. The triage says the camera was readable and the policy was at fault in those runs, so the flag most likely reflects the policy's own drift, not a rendering problem.

## Open

Repeats at 1.0x / 0 s on 048b974e, 07981e6a, 0caa8f1a, 0d4893f5, 18d28973 and 1bbe02fc would settle it. Failures there would mean the cases are route-following failures, not speed and timing cases.

- No run struck a pedestrian, so the question as asked (which combinations make the policy hit the pedestrian) remains unanswered. A search focused on the cleaner scenes 0e002edd, 18f8dbd6 and 0b10bce8 could answer it, using closest approach to the key pedestrian. 12a09194 was never run.
- The failure check cannot tell a pedestrian collision from a vehicle collision. Only the triage shows that no pedestrian was hit, so a pedestrian-specific collision measure is needed.
- Most confirmed failures are flagged as possible artifacts because the ego was more than 3.5 m off the recorded path. The triage blames the policy, but runs that check the rendering would rule out the simulator.
- The 1bbe02fc off-road flags at about 0.5 m from the recorded path may be map-boundary effects. Checking the drivable-area map at that junction would show whether those failures are real.
- Three repeats per confirmed case only show a failure rate above one half. More repeats would be needed to estimate the actual rates.

Traffic is replay, which keeps the scenario scripted as in Euro NCAP pedestrian tests. There is no delay, bias or noise.

Two other tagged scenes are left out: 095cf563 and 13a9767e. Neither has a pedestrian key actor, so a person-class retime there might hit no actor or the wrong one.

With 90% ranges, each confirmed case needs about 4–5 failures out of 5 repeats, so roughly 25 runs go to confirmation and about 25 to searching the 5×9 grid.
