# In pedestrian-crossing scenes, which combinations of ego speed at hand-off (0.6x–1.4x recorded) and pedestrian step-out time (±2 s) make the VaVAM policy with the linear MPC collide or leave the road? Find the 5 most challenging cases, each on a different scene, confirmed by repeats.

Study `random_confirm_r2`: 39 kept runs on 9 scenes, varying ego_speed_scale, actor_time_shift_s; 1 runs not kept by the gate. Counts are kept runs only.

**Goal (checked by code, not the model):** 5 of 5 challenging settings confirmed, after 4 rounds.

## Answer

The goal was met: five settings on five different scenes failed on every repeat. They are S2 (07981e6a, 0.6x, +1.5 s), S7 (0caa8f1a, 0.6x, +1 s), S10 (0d4893f5, 0.8x, +2 s), S16 (0e002edd, 1.4x, −1.5 s) and S22 (18d28973, 1.2x, −1 s). Each rests on only three runs, so the confirmed failure rate is only known to be somewhat above one half. None of these failures, and no failure anywhere in the study, involved the pedestrian. In every triaged run the policy left its route, usually drifting left even though the command was RIGHT. It then hit parked cars, drove head-on into oncoming traffic, ran onto a median or curb, or cut a right turn too tightly. So these are challenging cases for the policy, but the study does not show that the combination of ego speed and pedestrian timing causes them. On most of these scenes the policy also failed at other speeds and step-out times, and the nominal setting (1.0x, 0 s) was never run to check whether they fail anyway.
