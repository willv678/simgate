## Answer

The study's goal was met: it confirmed 5 challenging settings, each on a different scene. They are S2 (048b974e, 1.4x, −1 s), S12 (0e002edd, 1.4x, 0 s), S6 (0d4893f5, 1.2x, −0.5 s), S14 (18d28973, 1.2x, −0.5 s) and S21 (1bbe02fc, 1.2x, −0.5 s). However, none of the triaged failures involved the pedestrian. The policy drove into parked cars, sideswiped a car in the next lane, or left the road, usually by drifting sideways or not following the route command. So these are the hardest settings found, but not cases where speed and pedestrian timing cause a pedestrian strike. The study also never ran the nominal setting (1.0x, 0 s) on the failing scenes, so it cannot say whether the pedestrian's timing matters at all. Three cases (S6, S14, S21) rest on only a few repeats, and their ranges clear the 0.5 bar only narrowly. Some failures may not be clean policy errors. In S21 the off-road flags came only 0.5 m from the human's path and may be lane-edge or map-boundary artifacts. Every failure in S12 and S14 is marked as a possible artifact, although the video review found clean camera views and put the fault on the policy.
