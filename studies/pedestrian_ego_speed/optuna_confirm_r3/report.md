# In pedestrian-crossing scenes, which combinations of ego speed at hand-off (0.6x–1.4x recorded) and pedestrian step-out time (±2 s) make the VaVAM policy with the linear MPC collide or leave the road? Find the 5 most challenging cases, each on a different scene, confirmed by repeats.

Study `optuna_confirm_r3`: 40 kept runs on 10 scenes, varying ego_speed_scale, actor_time_shift_s; 0 runs not kept by the gate. Counts are kept runs only.

**Goal (checked by code, not the model):** 5 of 5 challenging settings confirmed, after 4 rounds.

## Answer

The goal check confirmed five failing settings, each on a different scene: S4, S7, S10, S17 and S20. But none of these failures involved the pedestrian, so the study does not show that any speed and step-out combination makes the policy hit a pedestrian. In every triaged failure the policy ignored a RIGHT route command and steered left. It hit parked cars, side-swiped a car in the next lane, drove into oncoming traffic, ran onto a median, or was flagged off-road at the stop line. The same scenes also failed at most other settings tried there, and the plan warned that a scene failing regardless of the knobs fills a slot without answering the question. Each confirmed case rests on three repeats whose 90% ranges only just clear 0.5. On top of that, S7 and S17 rest entirely on runs flagged as possible simulator artifacts (the ego ended far off the recorded path), and S20 rests on marginal off-road flags that may come from the map boundary.

## Open

- Whether any combination of ego speed and step-out time causes a collision with the pedestrian. No failure involved the pedestrian, and the failure check cannot tell which actor was hit. Rerunning with a check for collisions with the person class, plus closest approach to the retimed pedestrian, on the cleaner scenes (0e002edd, 18f8dbd6) would settle it.
- Whether the five confirmed scenes fail at nominal settings anyway. No run was made at 1.0x / 0 s on 07981e6a, 0caa8f1a, 0d4893f5, 18d28973 or 1bbe02fc. Three to five nominal repeats on each would show whether the knobs matter.
- Whether the 1bbe02fc off-road flags are real. They fire about 0.5 m from the human path, so the map boundary at that stop line needs checking. Otherwise this case should not count.
- Whether the S7 and S17 failures are the simulator's. All of their runs are flagged as possible artifacts, though triage blames the policy. Checking the rendering along the off-path trajectories would settle it.
- The confirmed rates rest on three repeats each. More repeats (for example five to eight) at S4, S7, S10, S17 and S20 would narrow the ranges. The partial result at S12 (0e002edd, 0.6x / +2.0 s) also needs more runs.
- The repeated left drift against RIGHT route commands across scenes suggests a problem with how the policy follows route commands, not with pedestrian timing. A study varying or checking the route command would test this.
