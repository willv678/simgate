SimGate study · rules_r3

In pedestrian-crossing scenes, which combinations of ego speed at hand-off (0.6x–1.4x recorded) and pedestrian step-out time (±2 s) make the VaVAM policy with the linear MPC collide or leave the road? Find the 5 most challenging cases, each on a different scene, confirmed by repeats.

31 simulated drives on 10 scene(s) rebuilt from real recordings, varying ego speed, actor time shift.

What the words on this page mean
  • Run: one simulated drive of a scene by the driving policy under test.
  • Scene: a few seconds of a real recorded drive, rebuilt so the policy can drive it again; named by the first characters of its id.
  • Test car (the "ego"): the car the policy drives. The other road users replay what they did in the recording.
  • Recorded drive: the path the human driver took in the recording.
  • Kept: the run passed every automatic check that the simulator ran it as asked. Only kept runs count as evidence.
  • Failed: the test car hit something with its front or side, or left the road.
  • Setting: one scene at one knob value, numbered S1, S2, … so the findings can cite it.
  • 90% range: the failure rates that fit what was seen, at 90% confidence. Few runs give a wide range.

1. The question

In pedestrian-crossing scenes, which combinations of ego speed at hand-off (0.6x–1.4x recorded) and pedestrian step-out time (±2 s) make the VaVAM policy with the linear MPC collide or leave the road? Find the 5 most challenging cases, each on a different scene, confirmed by repeats.

What was varied: ego speed: speed of the test car when the driving policy takes over, relative to its recording at that place; actor time shift: seconds by which the retimed road user moves later than recorded (negative: earlier).

The researcher's brief, as written

# Pedestrian crossing: ego speed against pedestrian timing ## Question In scenes where a pedestrian crosses or walks near the ego's path, which combinations of the ego's speed when the driving policy takes over and the pedestrian's step-out time make the policy hit the pedestrian or leave the road? Find the 5 most challenging cases, each on a different scene, confirmed by repeats. ## Scope Scenes tagged pedestrian_crossing. Vary the ego's speed at hand-off (0.6x to 1.4x its recorded speed) and retime the recorded pedestrians (class person): step out up to 2 s earlier or later, at their recorded walking speed. Replay the other traffic as recorded so the scenario is scripted, as in Euro NCAP pedestrian tests, where the test vehicle's speed and the pedestrian's timing are the two parameters. No delay or perception error. ## Budget About 50 runs. ## What counts A front or side collision, or leaving the road. Near misses rank next.

2. The answer

The win condition was met, but it does not answer the question. All five confirmed cases (S1, S2, S6, S12, S14) are at the nominal setting (1.0x speed, 0 s shift), and none involved the pedestrian. In every failure the policy steered left against a RIGHT or STRAIGHT route command. It then hit a parked, oncoming or adjacent car, or left the road. These scenes fail with no speed or timing change, so the study found no speed and step-out combinations that cause failures: no run moved the pedestrian's timing at all, and speed was only changed in two scenes, where nothing failed. Each confirmed case rests on only three repeats, so the ranges show the failure rate is above one half, not how close to certain it is. Two of the five are also soft. In S14 all three failures were marginal offroad flags close to the human's path, so they may come from the lane map. In S1, S6 and S12 every failure was flagged as a possible simulator artifact, although triage found the policy at fault with a clean camera view.

Goal, checked by code (not by the model): 5 of 5 challenging settings confirmed, after 3 rounds.

Still open

  • The study never moved the pedestrian's step-out time, so it is unknown whether any combination of speed and timing makes the policy hit the pedestrian. To find out, spend the unused budget, about 20 runs, on 0e002edd and 18f8dbd6. Cross speeds of 1.2x and 1.4x with shifts of −2 s to +2 s, then repeat any setting that fails or comes close.
  • Speeds above 1.0x were never tried in any scene.
  • Do the nominal failures in S1, S6 and S12 come from the policy or from rendering once the ego is far off the recorded path? Re-running them with the camera checked frame by frame, or under a different render setup, would settle it.
  • Are S14's offroad flags real or a lane-map artifact? A check of the drivable-area map at the stop line, or runs scored on collisions only, would settle it.
  • Why does the policy steer left against the route command in so many scenes? This looks like a route-command or conditioning fault worth checking on its own. It also means these scenes can't test the pedestrian question until that fault is fixed.
  • The pedestrian scenes 0b10bce8 and 12a09194 have one run per setting and no timing shifts, so they were barely explored.

7. Where this came from

The plan was written by claude-opus-5-5 from the brief and checked by code against the knob catalog and the run budget. The runs were chosen in 3 rounds by rules; code rejected any choice outside the plan before it ran. Failures were explained by claude-opus-5-5. The answer and findings were written by claude-opus-5-5.

Runs: 31 in the study, 30 kept, 1 not kept; 18 of the kept runs failed.

How the numbers were computed: a run counts only if it passed every automatic check of the simulator (it is kept). A run failed if the test car hit something with its front or side, or left the road. For each setting, code counts the kept runs that failed and gives the 90% Wilson range of the failure rate. A failure is flagged as possibly the simulator's when the test car was more than 3.5 m (about a lane) from the recorded drive, or a camera frame was black, because there the rebuilt scene is drawn less reliably. The model wrote the words; every count and range on this page is computed from the run records, not written by the model.

Why the plan looks the way it does

Scenes: these are the 10 scenes tagged pedestrian_crossing that have a recorded pedestrian key actor. Each scene's pedestrian is set in retime_tracks, so actor_time_shift_s moves only that person. actor_speed_scale stays at 1.0, so the pedestrian walks at the recorded speed. Traffic is replay, which keeps the scenario scripted as in Euro NCAP pedestrian tests. There is no delay, bias or noise. Two other tagged scenes are left out: 095cf563 and 13a9767e. Neither has a pedestrian key actor, so a person-class retime there might hit no actor or the wrong one. Budget: 5 rounds of 10 runs gives 50 runs, as the brief asks. The goal is top_k with k=5, high_min=0.5 and distinct scenes. With 90% ranges, each confirmed case needs about 4–5 failures out of 5 repeats, so roughly 25 runs go to confirmation and about 25 to searching the 5×9 grid. That is tight, and with partial results the study may confirm fewer than 5 cases. Caveats: - 6 of the scenes (048b974e, 07981e6a, 0caa8f1a, 0d4893f5, 18d28973, 1bbe02fc) failed at 0 delay in an earlier CATK run. That run used reactive traffic, not replay, so it may not carry over. If one still fails at 1.0x / 0 s, its failures may not come from speed or timing, and it fills a top_k slot cheaply without answering the question. - 0e002edd, 18f8dbd6, 12a09194 and 0b10bce8 are the cleaner tests. In 0b10bce8 the ego barely moved (8.9 m), and in 12a09194 the pedestrian stayed far away (11.5 m). - The failure check counts any front or side collision or leaving the road. It cannot tell whether the ego hit the pedestrian or another actor. Near misses are reported as closest approach to any actor, not ranked as failures. - With ego_speed_scale above 1, the first second or two of the warm-up starts off the recorded scene.
